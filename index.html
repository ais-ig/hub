Two years, one programme

The big picture, from Grade 9 through to A-Level

Grades 9 and 10 are not two separate years. They form a single continuous IGCSE programme, and the subjects chosen at the start of Grade 9 carry all the way through to the external examinations at the end of Grade 10.

A-Levels and beyond

AS then A2, built directly on the IGCSE subjects taken, and recognised by universities worldwide.

Cambridge or Edexcel Mathematics

Same content, same standard. The difference is timing and calculators.

What the two boards share

  • Both prepare students for advanced level study.
  • Designed by subject experts and based on the latest educational research.
  • Extensive teaching and learning resources, and internationally recognised qualifications.
  • The exam: two papers, two hours each, 200 marks in total.

Where they differ

Cambridge
  • Both papers must be taken in the same session.
  • Calculator allowed in one paper only.
Edexcel
  • Modular. One paper can be sat in one session and the other in a separate session.
  • Calculator allowed in both papers: Paper 1H and Paper 2H, 100 marks each.

Reading the options form

  • Table A lists the subjects every student takes. There is nothing to choose here.
  • Table B holds the choices, set out as either/or pairs. Tick one subject in each row.
  • Table C, on the Grade 9 form only, is an optional tick box for the Hifdh Programme.
  • Whichever combination you tick, the week comes to 40 periods.
  • Sign the form and return it by the deadline printed on it.

How marks are built

Continuous and summative assessment, quarter by quarter

Each semester is worth fifty per cent of the year. Within a semester, marks come from continuous assessment gathered through both quarters and from summative assessments at fixed points.

Semester Two repeats this pattern exactly, across Quarters 3 and 4. The two semesters together make the year.

How progress reaches you

  • Reports are issued at the end of each quarter and at the end of each semester.
  • Twice a semester you receive a results and support plan by email, subject by subject, with what we are doing next.
  • An overall score below sixty per cent in any subject triggers the intervention described under Support.

How we keep track of your child

Support and intervention

Mock exams

Full exam simulations under real conditions, so the summer session is not the first time it feels real.

Progress sheets

Every subject keeps a Student Academic Progress Sheet, tracking individual growth through the year.

Early support

Anyone slipping below 60% is picked up during the term and given a named intervention plan. You receive a written summary of the affected subjects, individual interviews are held with the student, and a tailored support plan follows.

In short You will hear from us before the grade drops, not after.

Homeroom Mentorship Programme

Every student has a homeroom mentor who follows their academic progress, well-being and development across the year.

  • Weekly class check-ins. Ten to fifteen minutes in homeroom time, covering group well-being, academic updates, behaviour expectations, shared concerns and achievements.
  • Monthly one-to-one sessions. At least once a month, ten to fifteen minutes per student, reviewing academic performance, emotional and social well-being, and goals for the term ahead.
  • Private conversations whenever a student needs one.
  • Mentor appointments with parents. Short scheduled meetings through the semester to talk through progress and next steps.

What we ask of students and families

A positive and safe learning environment

Attendance

  • Daily attendance is essential to academic success, and absence shows up in results faster than most families expect.
  • Absence and early dismissal requests must be made through MyAIS only. Requests by phone, WhatsApp or in person cannot be processed.

Mobile phones

  • Phones are not permitted during the school day, lessons, breaks and lunchtimes included, and must be stored in lockers.
  • A confiscated phone is returned to a parent or guardian in person, not to a driver or any other representative, and only once the commitment form is signed.
  • Confiscation periods increase with each repeat.
  • Where violations continue, the student is referred to the counsellor and parents are asked to come in.

Behaviour and community

Respect for teachers, classmates and the school. Responsibility for the work, the deadline and the outcome. Islamic values as the foundation of our community. Where behaviour falls short, three levels guide the response, so students and families always know what happens next.

Level A · Minor
Uniform, low-level disruption.
Response: teacher redirection and reflection time, with parent contact if it persists.
Level B · Repeated or disruptive
Inappropriate language, misuse of property.
Response: counsellor referral and a behavioural contract, with Head of School involvement if repeated.
Level C · Serious safety concern
Violence, bullying, leaving school grounds.
Response: Deputy Head referral, safeguarding log, parent meetings, and possible suspension or alternative placement.
